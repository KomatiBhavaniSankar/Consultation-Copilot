# Stroke: clinical quick reference
(Starter note. Verify against your local stroke protocol.)

- Two main types: ischaemic (about 85%, blocked artery) and haemorrhagic (bleeding). They look the same at the bedside; a brain scan separates them.
- Establish last-known-well time. Check capillary glucose, because hypoglycaemia can mimic stroke.
- Non-contrast CT head as soon as possible. Targets: imaging within about 20 to 25 minutes of arrival.
- Ischaemic stroke within 4.5 hours of onset without contraindication: IV thrombolysis. Door-to-needle target 60 minutes or less.
- Large-vessel occlusion on CT angiography: mechanical thrombectomy, which can help up to 24 hours in selected patients.
- Blood pressure: before thrombolysis keep below 185/110. Without thrombolysis, treat only if above 220/120.
- Nil by mouth until a swallow screen is passed.
- Give oxygen only if SpO2 is below 94%.
- Risk factors to ask about: hypertension, atrial fibrillation, diabetes, smoking, high cholesterol, previous TIA.
