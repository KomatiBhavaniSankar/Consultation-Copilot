# Stroke: emergency steps for bystanders and families
(Starter note. Replace or extend with your reviewed documents.)

## Recognise a stroke: BE-FAST
- Balance: sudden loss of balance or dizziness.
- Eyes: sudden loss of vision in one or both eyes, or double vision.
- Face: one side of the face droops; the smile is uneven.
- Arm: weakness or numbness of one arm or leg, usually one side of the body.
- Speech: slurred, confused or lost speech; cannot understand others.
- Time: if any sign is present, call emergency services immediately.
Other signs: sudden severe headache with no known cause, sudden confusion, trouble walking.

## What to do
1. Call the emergency number immediately and say "possible stroke".
2. Note the time symptoms started, or the last time the person was seen normal. This decides treatment options.
3. Keep the person calm, seated or lying with head and shoulders slightly raised. Loosen tight clothing.
4. Give nothing to eat or drink. Do not give aspirin or other medicines unless the emergency doctor says so. About 1 in 7 strokes is caused by bleeding in the brain, and aspirin can make bleeding worse.
5. If unconscious but breathing, place in the recovery position (on the side). If not breathing normally, start CPR.
6. Stay with the person. Tell the ambulance crew the time of onset, medicines (especially blood thinners), and medical history.
7. Go to a hospital that has a CT scanner and a stroke team. An ambulance can alert the hospital in advance.

## Do not
- Do not wait to see if symptoms go away. Short-lived symptoms (a transient ischaemic attack, TIA) are still an emergency.
- Do not drive the person yourself if an ambulance is available.
- Do not give food, water or tablets.
