# After birth: care and postpartum haemorrhage
(Starter note. Verify against your hospital protocol.)

- Postpartum haemorrhage (PPH): blood loss of 1000 mL or more, or any loss with signs of low blood volume. Causes: tone (uterus not contracting), trauma, retained tissue, thrombin (clotting).
- First actions: call for help, massage the uterus, give oxytocin, empty the bladder, start IV fluids, tranexamic acid 1 g IV within 3 hours of birth, check for tears and retained placenta.
- Warn mothers to seek help for heavy bleeding, fever, severe headache, leg swelling or pain, chest pain, breathlessness, or low mood that does not lift.
- Routine checks: blood pressure, bleeding, uterine tone, perineum, passing urine, baby feeding and temperature.
- Baby danger signs: fast or difficult breathing, not feeding, fever or very low temperature, yellow skin in the first day, floppy or very sleepy.
