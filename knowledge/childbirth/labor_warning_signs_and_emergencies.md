# Labor warning signs and obstetric emergencies
(Starter note. Verify against your hospital protocol.)

- Heavy vaginal bleeding (soaking a pad in an hour) or painful constant abdominal pain: possible placental abruption or placenta previa. Urgent senior help.
- Severe headache, visual disturbance, upper abdominal pain or swelling: possible pre-eclampsia. Blood pressure 140/90 or more is raised; 160/110 or more is severe and needs urgent treatment. Seizure means eclampsia: magnesium sulfate, protect airway.
- Waters break with green or brown fluid (meconium) or before 37 weeks: needs prompt assessment.
- Reduced baby movements or abnormal fetal heart rate: urgent assessment.
- Visible or felt umbilical cord (cord prolapse): knee-chest position, relieve pressure on the cord, emergency caesarean.
- Shoulder dystocia (head delivered, shoulders stuck): call for help, McRoberts manoeuvre, suprapubic pressure.
- Fever of 38 C or more in labor: possible infection, assess for antibiotics.
- Labor that does not progress, or mother exhausted or distressed: senior review.
