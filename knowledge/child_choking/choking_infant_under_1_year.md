# Choking in a baby under 1 year
(Starter note. Replace or extend with your reviewed documents.)

Signs of severe choking: cannot cry, cough or breathe; silent; weak or no cough; face turning blue or grey.
If the baby is coughing strongly or crying, they are moving air. Let them cough and stay close.

## Steps
1. Shout for help. Call the emergency number (or put the phone on speaker).
2. Back blows: hold the baby face down along your forearm, resting on your thigh, head lower than the chest. Support the jaw, not the soft throat. Give up to 5 firm blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of the other hand.
3. Chest thrusts: turn the baby face up, head lower than the body. Use two fingers in the centre of the chest, just below the nipple line. Give up to 5 quick thrusts, about one third of the chest depth.
4. Look in the mouth. Remove an object only if you can see it. Never sweep blindly with a finger.
5. Repeat 5 back blows and 5 chest thrusts until the object comes out.
6. If the baby becomes unresponsive: start infant CPR (30 compressions with two fingers, then 2 gentle breaths). Keep the phone on speaker.
7. Take the baby to a doctor even if the object comes out.

## Never
- No abdominal thrusts (Heimlich) on a baby under 1 year.
- Do not hold the baby upside down by the legs or shake.
