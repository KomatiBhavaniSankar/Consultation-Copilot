# Heart attack (acute coronary syndrome)
(Starter note. Verify against your local protocol.)

## Recognise
Chest pressure or pain for more than a few minutes, spreading to arm, jaw, neck or back; breathlessness, cold sweat, nausea. Women, older adults and people with diabetes may have only breathlessness, tiredness or indigestion.

## First aid
Call emergency services. Rest, half-sitting. A chewed adult aspirin (300 to 325 mg) if not allergic and the dispatcher or doctor agrees. Do not let the person walk or drive. Be ready for CPR and an AED.

## Clinicians
- 12-lead ECG within 10 minutes of arrival. Monitor, defibrillator pads, IV access.
- STEMI: primary PCI, first medical contact to device within 90 minutes (120 if transfer). If PCI cannot be done within 120 minutes, fibrinolysis within 30 minutes of arrival.
- Aspirin 162 to 325 mg chewed unless contraindicated. Nitrates for ongoing pain if not hypotensive, no right ventricular infarct and no recent PDE-5 inhibitor. Oxygen only if SpO2 below 90%.
- Serial high-sensitivity troponin when the ECG is not diagnostic.
- Think of aortic dissection, pulmonary embolism and pericarditis before anticoagulation.
